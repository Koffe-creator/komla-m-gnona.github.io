01 About

I'm a computational biologist with a PhD from The Ohio State University, where I developed statistical methodology for case-control studies — work that ended up peer-reviewed in the American Journal of Epidemiology.

Since then I've spent my time where the data is messiest and the stakes are highest: multi-conditional single-cell RNA-seq and Perturb-seq screens on iPSC-derived human neurons, multi-omics integration for immune-mediated disease, and machine-learning models that classify compound mechanisms of action. At Sensorium Therapeutics, that modeling work fed directly into prioritization decisions on a program now in Phase 1 clinical trials.

I care a lot about the unglamorous parts — reproducible Nextflow pipelines, honest evaluation on imbalanced data, and code a colleague can read six months later. I work best sitting between the wet lab and the cluster, translating what came off the instrument into something a team can act on.

03 Selected projects

Self-directed work on public datasets. Each one is small enough to read end-to-end and answers a question I actually wanted answered.

Single-cell · Perturbation

Perturb-seq screens ranked by E-distance

CRISPR screen analysis with pertpy: Mixscape to separate true knockouts from escapers, then E-distance to rank perturbation effect size and an E-test for significance. On the Papalexi ECCITE-seq screen the top hits recover the IFN-γ/JAK-STAT axis — STAT1, JAK2, IFNGR1/2, IRF1 — with correct STAT1 specificity. Extended to a genetic-interaction table comparing double perturbations against their singles to call synergy vs. suppression.

  • pertpy
  • scanpy
  • Mixscape
  • E-distance

Single-cell · Deep learning

Batch integration benchmark: PCA vs. Harmony vs. scVI

Benchmarked three integration strategies on the public heart cell atlas using batch-mixing entropy and cell-type silhouette. Harmony wins on mixing, scVI preserves more biology, and both beat raw PCA — a real trade-off rather than a winner. Then used scANVI for label transfer, reaching 98% accuracy on held-out cells, with the errors concentrated in rare and transcriptionally similar types.

  • scvi-tools
  • Harmony
  • PyTorch
  • scANVI

LLM · Retrieval

RAG over the atopic dermatitis literature

Retrieval-augmented QA on real PubMed abstracts: TF-IDF retrieval, cited answers, and a hallucination guard that refuses when the retrieved context doesn't support a claim. Evaluated in two tiers — Recall@k and MRR for the retriever, then LLM-as-judge faithfulness and answer-relevancy for the generator.

  • RAG
  • DeepEval
  • PubMed API
  • Python

Variant interpretation

AlphaMissense benchmarked against ClinVar

Scored AlphaMissense pathogenicity predictions against ClinVar labels across ten disease genes. Headline ROC-AUC is 0.944 — but the interesting result is the failure mode: NPC1 I1061T, a common and firmly pathogenic Niemann-Pick C1 mutation, scores 0.303 and is called benign. A reminder that aggregate metrics hide the misses that matter clinically.

  • MyVariant.info
  • ClinVar
  • ROC analysis

Cheminformatics · ML

Predicting CNS indication from chemical structure

Random-forest classifier predicting whether a compound targets a mental-health indication, straight from SMILES. Compared seven interpretable RDKit descriptors against 2048-bit Morgan fingerprints under 10-fold CV with explicit class-imbalance handling. The top descriptors turn out to be a blood-brain-barrier profile — logP, MW, TPSA — which is exactly what you'd expect, since a CNS drug has to get across.

  • RDKit
  • scikit-learn
  • Random Forest

Computer vision

Skin-lesion classification with a small CNN

A compact PyTorch CNN on DermaMNIST with a learning-rate schedule and best-model checkpointing, scored on AUC, balanced accuracy, and macro-F1 rather than raw accuracy — the dataset is badly imbalanced. The tiny network reaches 0.893 AUC against a benchmark ResNet-18 at 0.917.

  • PyTorch
  • CNN
  • MedMNIST
